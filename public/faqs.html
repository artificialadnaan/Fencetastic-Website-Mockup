General Fence Questions

General Fence FAQs

Everything you need to know about choosing, installing, and maintaining your fence.

Aluminum or vinyl is likely to be the most durable. These materials are resistant to rot, decay, and insect damage and can withstand high winds and heavy rain. Aluminum and vinyl require minimal upkeep and don't need painting or treatment. Wrought iron also withstands extreme weather but may require rust protection maintenance.
Top choices include cedar, redwood, and pressure-treated pine. Cedar is a naturally rot-resistant wood that has a warm, reddish-brown color and a straight grain. Redwood offers similar benefits with strength and durability. Pressure-treated pine is a more economical alternative. All wood fences require regular maintenance including staining and sealing for protection.
Fence posts should be buried deep enough to ensure stability and prevent them from tipping over. The general rule of thumb is to bury them at least 2-3 feet deep. Depth varies by soil type, climate, fence height and weight. Holes should be 6-8 inches in diameter, wider at the bottom for drainage. Posts must be verified plumb with a level.
A well-built and well-maintained wood fence can last between 15 to 20 years. Cedar and redwood can last 20-25 years with proper care. Pressure-treated pine lasts 15-20 years. Regular maintenance through staining, sealing, inspections and repairs significantly extends longevity.
Steps include: inspecting regularly for loose boards, cracks, rot or insect damage; cleaning with mild detergent and soft brush; applying sealant or stain; tightening loose boards and nails; fixing rot or damage with wood preservative; monitoring weather-related damage. Neglecting maintenance shortens lifespan and causes instability.
Recommendations include using rot-resistant wood like cedar, redwood or pressure-treated wood; installing proper drainage systems; regular maintenance and cleaning; keeping fence dry by removing debris; installing gravel boards at base; avoiding overcrowded plants; treating wood with quality preservatives before installation; using galvanized or stainless-steel fasteners.
Vinyl fences are resistant to rot, warping, and insect damage, which means they require very little maintenance. They don't require painting or staining and resist fading, scratches and dents. Vinyl is easily cleaned with hose and mild detergent, available in multiple colors and styles, and made from recyclable materials without toxic chemicals.
Ornamental Fencing

Ornamental Fence FAQs

Learn about ornamental fence types, benefits, and care.

Ornamental fencing is a type of fencing that is designed to add both function and aesthetic appeal to a property. Made from aluminum, wrought iron, and steel, it features elegant patterns and intricate details. It's durable, rust-resistant, long-lasting, and versatile for various purposes from privacy to property marking.
Types include: Aluminum (affordable, lightweight, rust-resistant); Wrought iron (elegant, classic, requires maintenance); Steel (strong, available in galvanized and powder-coated finishes); Vinyl (low-maintenance, multiple styles and colors); Wood (natural, rustic look, requires regular maintenance). Selection depends on durability, maintenance needs, design preferences and budget.
Maintenance involves regular cleaning with mild detergent and soft brush; periodic painting or powder-coating for metal fencing to prevent rust; rust prevention measures; checking after severe weather. Specific requirements depend on fencing material type and property location.
