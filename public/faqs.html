Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about fencing materials, installation, maintenance, gates, decks, pergolas, and more.

Aluminum or vinyl fences perform best in extreme conditions. These materials resist rot, decay, and insect damage while withstanding high winds and heavy rain. They require minimal upkeep and need no painting or treatment. Wrought iron also endures extreme weather but demands more maintenance for rust protection.
Posts should be buried at least 2-3 feet deep, though depth varies by soil type and climate. Loose soil or windy areas may require deeper installation. Holes should measure 6-8 inches in diameter, wider at the base for drainage. Always use a level to ensure posts remain plumb and fencing stays level.
Cedar, redwood, and pressure-treated pine are ideal choices. Cedar offers natural rot resistance with warm coloring and lightweight workability. Redwood provides strength and durability with similar aesthetics. Pressure-treated pine costs less while resisting rot and insects. All wood requires regular staining, sealing, and maintenance to protect from moisture and sun damage.
Well-maintained wood fences typically last 15-20 years. Cedar and redwood extend to 20-25 years with proper care. Pressure-treated pine lasts about 15-20 years. Lifespan depends on wood quality, installation standards, climate, and soil conditions. Regular maintenance through staining, sealing, and repairs significantly extends longevity.
Inspect regularly for loose boards, cracks, rot, or insect damage. Clean with mild detergent and soft brush; rinse thoroughly and dry completely. Apply sealant or stain evenly using brush or roller. Tighten loose fasteners and replace damaged boards. Address rot with wood preservative and replacement materials. Check for storm damage and weather impacts. Consult professionals for complex repairs.
Use naturally rot-resistant woods like cedar, redwood, or pressure-treated lumber. Install proper drainage systems for ventilation. Maintain through regular inspection, cleaning, and protective coating application. Keep debris and overhanging branches away from the fence. Install gravel boards or concrete footings to prevent soil contact. Avoid overcrowding surrounding plants. Apply quality wood preservative before installation. Use hot-dipped galvanized or stainless-steel fasteners.
Vinyl fencing, made from PVC plastic, resists rot, warping, and insect damage with minimal maintenance. Unlike wood, vinyl requires no painting or staining and won't fade over time. It resists scratches and dents while maintaining appearance for years. Cleaning requires only a hose and mild detergent. Vinyl comes in multiple colors and styles, mimics wood aesthetics, and represents an eco-friendly choice using recycled, recyclable materials.
Vinyl fencing typically lasts 20-30 years or more with minimal maintenance. It resists the common issues that shorten wood fence lifespan such as rot, insect damage, and weather-related deterioration. Many manufacturers offer lifetime warranties on vinyl fencing products.
Yes, modern vinyl fencing is formulated with UV inhibitors that prevent fading, chalking, and brittleness from sun exposure. Quality vinyl fencing maintains its color and structural integrity even in the intense Texas summer heat. Look for vinyl products that are specifically rated for UV resistance.
Ornamental fencing combines function with aesthetic appeal using materials like aluminum, wrought iron, and steel. It features elegant, intricate patterns and details that add sophistication to properties. Available in various colors through painting or powder-coating, it's durable and rust-resistant for outdoor use. Versatile applications include privacy screening, boundary marking, and pet/child safety across small yards to large estates.
Five main types exist: Aluminum (affordable, lightweight, rust-resistant, low-maintenance); Wrought Iron (elegant, durable, classic appearance, requires regular maintenance); Steel (strong, durable, available galvanized or powder-coated); Vinyl (low-maintenance PVC, available in traditional and contemporary styles); Wood (natural, rustic appearance, requires regular maintenance). Each offers unique characteristics; selection depends on durability needs, maintenance tolerance, design preferences, and budget.
Cleaning is essential - use mild detergent and soft-bristled brush to remove accumulated dirt and grime, then rinse thoroughly. Metal fencing may require periodic painting or powder-coating for rust protection every few years depending on product used. Rust prevention through protective coatings extends fencing lifespan. Regular inspections identify needed repairs early.
Horse fencing requires high visibility and smooth surfaces to prevent injury. Centaur/Cenflex vinyl rail fencing is a top choice as it's flexible, visible, and won't splinter. Wood board fencing (3-rail or 4-rail) is a traditional option. Avoid barbed wire and high-tensile wire as these can cause serious horse injuries. The fence should be at least 54-60 inches tall for most horse breeds.
Height depends on the livestock being contained. Cattle typically need 48-54 inch fencing. Horses require 54-60 inches minimum. Goats need at least 48 inches with tight spacing to prevent climbing. Deer fencing should be 8 feet or taller. Dogs vary by breed but generally 4-6 feet. Always check local regulations for minimum height requirements.
Common farm fence materials include wood (traditional, attractive but requires maintenance), woven wire (versatile for multiple livestock types), welded wire (rigid panels, good for smaller animals), pipe/steel (extremely durable for cattle), vinyl rail (low-maintenance, safe for horses), and electric (effective psychological barrier, lower cost). The best choice depends on your specific livestock, property size, and budget.
Automatic gates offer enhanced security through controlled access with keypads, remotes, or cameras. They provide convenience by eliminating the need to manually open/close gates, especially in bad weather. They enhance property value and curb appeal. Automatic gates also improve safety by keeping children and pets contained while controlling who enters the property.
Fencetastic installs several professional-grade gate operators. Swing gate operators (like the CSW24UL) handle gates up to 18 feet or 1,600 lbs. Slide gate operators (like the CSL24UL) manage gates up to 50 feet and 1,500 lbs. The Patriot II is a mechanical dual gate opener with 12-second cycle time. The Patriot RSL slide operator opens at 1 foot per second for gates up to 32 feet and 800 pounds. All include battery backup and solar compatibility options.
Sliding gates are ideal for properties with limited space, sloped driveways, or where the gate needs to span a wide opening. They're also generally safer as they don't swing outward into traffic or people. Swing gates are better for standard-width driveways with adequate clearance space. They offer a more traditional look and are typically less expensive to install. Consider your driveway slope, available space, and aesthetic preferences when choosing.
Composite decking (like Trex) is ideal for Texas. Made from 95% recycled materials, it resists rot, warping, splintering, and fading. It requires minimal maintenance - just occasional cleaning. Cedar is a good wood option due to natural insect and rot resistance. Pressure-treated pine is budget-friendly but needs regular maintenance. Stone decking offers lifetime durability and zero maintenance but costs more upfront.
Trex and similar composite decking products typically last 25-50 years with minimal maintenance. Most come with 25-year warranties against structural damage, fading, and staining. Unlike wood, composite doesn't need staining, sealing, or painting. Occasional cleaning with soap and water is all that's required.
Most cities in the DFW area require permits for deck construction, especially for elevated decks or those attached to the house. Permit requirements vary by city - McKinney, Frisco, Plano, Dallas, and other cities each have their own building codes. Fencetastic handles the permit process for all deck projects to ensure full compliance with local regulations.
A pergola has an open-beam roof structure with rafters that allow filtered sunlight through. It provides partial shade and is primarily decorative, creating an outdoor living space. A patio cover has a solid roof (flat, gabled, or shingled) that provides full protection from sun and rain. Patio covers can be attached to the house or freestanding. The choice depends on whether you want full weather protection or an aesthetic outdoor structure.
Cedar is the most popular pergola material in DFW due to its natural beauty, decay resistance, and insect resistance. Standard cedar pergolas include posts, beams, rafters, and runners. Vinyl pergolas are also available in traditional or modern designs, requiring virtually zero maintenance - just clean with soap and water. Customization options include post framing, angled brace arms, and stone post footings.
A well-built cedar pergola typically lasts 15-20 years with proper maintenance, and potentially longer with regular staining or sealing. Cedar naturally resists decay and insects, making it ideal for outdoor structures in the Texas climate. Vinyl pergolas can last 25-30 years or more with minimal maintenance.
